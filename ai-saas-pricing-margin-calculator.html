How to use the price floor

The price floor is the subscription price that would meet your target gross margin under the assumptions you entered. It includes AI usage, other per-customer variable costs, the percentage payment fee, and one fixed transaction fee per monthly payment. It does not mean that price is right for your market: customer value, competitors, support needs and willingness to pay still matter.

Minimum price = (AI cost per customer + other variable cost per customer + fixed payment fee) ÷ (1 − target gross margin − payment fee rate)

If the target margin plus the percentage payment fee is 100% or more, the model cannot find a finite price that meets that target. In that case, reduce the target or change the cost assumptions.

Gross margin is not the same as operating profit

Gross profit in this calculator means subscription revenue minus variable costs: AI API usage, other per-customer costs and processing fees. Operating profit then subtracts the monthly fixed-cost amount you enter. A product can have a positive gross margin and still lose money if the customer count is too low to cover fixed costs.

Break-even customers are estimated by dividing monthly fixed costs by contribution per customer. The result is rounded up to a whole customer. If contribution per customer is zero or negative, more customers will not cover fixed costs under the current assumptions.

Model variable AI usage, not only average usage

Averages can hide customers who send many more requests than expected. First estimate typical API cost using real token usage, then test higher usage, a heavier user mix, or an AI-cost increase. The stress test on this page doubles the per-customer AI cost while holding other inputs constant; it is a simple sensitivity check, not a prediction.

  • Measure input and output tokens separately for each important feature.
  • Include the chat history or retrieved context your application actually sends to the model.
  • Use limits or usage-based plans when a small number of power users could consume a disproportionate share of the API budget.
  • Recalculate after changing prompts, models, caching, payment providers, or subscription packaging.

Frequently asked questions

Does this guarantee my product will be profitable?

No. It models the costs you enter. Actual profit also depends on real usage, customer retention, refunds, taxes, acquisition costs, support, salaries, and many other business factors.

Are the payment fees current?

No. Payment processing values are editable assumptions, not live fee data. Use the rate and fixed transaction fee that apply to your country, provider, payment method and contract.

What if the minimum price is higher than competitors' prices?

Review the usage assumptions and value proposition. Possible responses include improving efficiency, limiting high-cost usage, offering separate plans, charging for overages, or testing a different model. Do not assume a lower subscription price is sustainable until the full costs are modeled.

Does the tool send my financial inputs to a server?

No. Calculations run in this page in your browser; the tool does not call a model or payment processor.
